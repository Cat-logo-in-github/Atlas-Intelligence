# Gradient Descent

### #Hook
"Imagine you're standing at the top of a mountain, with your flashlight only illuminating where you are right now. How do you find the lowest point? You take small steps in the direction that looks like it will lead down."

### #Script
I’m talking about how machines learn from their mistakes—the core principle behind many AI systems. Imagine standing at the top of a mountain, but with your flashlight only illuminating the ground around you.

How do you find the lowest point? You take small steps in the direction that looks like it will lead down.

This is what Gradient Descent does: an optimization algorithm that adjusts tiny numbers to gradually move a model toward better predictions. Instead of walking through visible terrain, we’re navigating a mathematical landscape of loss called the “loss surface.” 

Think of each update as you take a small step downhill—each time, moving slightly in the direction it looks like will make errors smaller and reduce the overall error.

The gradient tells us the steepness of this landscape. By following the steepest path down, we’re always getting closer to making better predictions.

Now imagine if your flashlight could illuminate more than a few feet ahead—you’d be able to see farther and take bigger steps toward reaching the bottom faster!

And that’s exactly what learning in AI systems is doing. They're taking many small steps following gradients to reduce error—just like you'd do with your flashlight on a mountain.
